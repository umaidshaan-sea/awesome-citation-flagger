1 Introduction 



In recommendation systems  and the Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, semantic details and  inside citations are used  to recommend new pieces of academic research papers [1, 2]. However, there is no indication on whether a paper is up to date based on similarity. Papers may still have a high similarity but be retracted or have major issues with methodology or fraud [3]. When recommending these papers to the top of search results the user is being provided with tainted scientific information. Scientometric analyses have shown that retracted papers continue to receive positive citations long after a notice of retraction [4]. A large reason for this is the inconsistencies in retraction meta data across the discovery platforms [5]. Within an autonomous system there would be a hidden false negative due to no machine readable retraction notice.
