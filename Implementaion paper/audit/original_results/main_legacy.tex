\begin{abstract}
AI-assisted literature discovery, neural citation recommenders, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines optimize almost exclusively for topical or embedding relevance, treating scholarly validity and publication status as constants. Consequently, retracted publications, uncorrected errors, and discredited empirical claims continue to be recommended and cited affirmatively by downstream generative systems. In this work, we present an automated, evidence-grounded computational framework that audits and reconciles heterogeneous, distributed retraction metadata across scholarly infrastructures to protect scientific citation integrity. Evaluating single-source baselines—specifically the OpenAlex Works API (\texttt{is\_retracted} Boolean, Baseline $B_0$) and the Crossref REST API (\texttt{updated-by} typed relation, Baseline $B_1$)—against a proposed provenance-aware multi-source consensus pipeline ($M_{\text{prop}}$) on a frozen benchmark of 80 articles ($N=80$: 40 formal retractions spanning Nature, Science, Elsevier, Wiley, Springer, PLOS, and IEEE, paired with 40 negative control publications), we reveal that OpenAlex achieves 100.0\% binary recall ($\text{F1}_{\text{e2e}}=1.000$) while Crossref suffers from a severe 40.0\% under-detection gap ($\text{Recall}=0.600$, $\text{F1}_{\text{e2e}}=0.750$) due to voluntary publisher deposit omissions. Furthermore, apparent multi-platform redundancy is an illusion, as both platforms depend on Retraction Watch. Our consensus pipeline matches the maximal 100.0\% recall of OpenAlex while attaching verifiable notice DOIs for 60.0\% of cases and enforcing safe epistemic abstention (\texttt{UNKNOWN}) on missing records. We provide a complete, reproducible software repository, deterministic protocol freezes, unit test suites, publication-grade figures, and actionable architectural recommendations for academic recommendation and RAG pipelines.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{\textit{Keywords}}---Citation Recommendation, Retraction Verification, Crossref, OpenAlex, RAG Safety, Epistemic Integrity.

\section{Introduction}
Neural citation recommenders and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems retrieve scholarly literature based on semantic embeddings and citation graphs \cite{jatowt2023mining, cohan2019structural}. However, modern retrieval architectures harbor a dangerous systemic blind spot: \emph{they conflate semantic relevance with scientific validity}. A recommended paper may be semantically and topically aligned with a user query while being scientifically invalid due to formal retraction, severe methodological errors, or fraud \cite{brainard2018rethinking}. When an AI recommendation system ranks a retracted paper at top positions based on similarity, it actively facilitates the propagation of contaminated science.

Empirical studies in scientometrics demonstrate that retracted papers continue to accumulate uncritical, affirmative citations decades after notice publication \cite{heibi2021qualitative}. A primary driver of this persistence is the fragmented availability of retraction metadata across digital discovery platforms \cite{frampton2021inconsistent}. In autonomous AI systems, missing machine-readable retraction tags generate silent false negatives. This study provides a rigorous empirical audit of this vulnerability.

\subsection{Research Questions and Hypotheses}
We formulate three pre-registered research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Detection Efficacy):} Does a provenance-aware consensus pipeline combining Crossref relations and OpenAlex indicators improve recall and end-to-end F1 over single-source baselines on a verified benchmark?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Under-Detection Gap):} To what extent does querying Crossref alone introduce silent false negatives, and what publisher-level factors explain this disparity?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Provenance and Proof):} How does shared Retraction Watch provenance impact multi-source validity, and can the proposed pipeline supply verifiable notice DOIs rather than an opaque Boolean?
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):} Combining Crossref and OpenAlex does not improve retraction detection recall over the best single-source comparator on the evaluation benchmark ($\text{Recall}_{M_{\text{prop}}} \le \max(\text{Recall}_{B0}, \text{Recall}_{B1})$).

\subsection{Summary of Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tractable Scope:} Operationalizes outdatedness from our base review into deterministic retraction classification with verifiable DOIs.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Comparative Framework:} Formulates Baseline $B_0$ (OpenAlex), Baseline $B_1$ (Crossref), and Proposed Consensus ($M_{\text{prop}}$).
    \item \textbf{Frozen 80-DOI Benchmark:} Curates a balanced evaluation split of 80 real scholarly articles (40 retractions across 7 major publishers, 40 landmark controls; seed \texttt{20261028}).
    \item \textbf{Provenance Audit:} Exposes the shared Retraction Watch dependency between OpenAlex and Crossref.
    \item \textbf{Safe Abstention and Dual F1:} Demonstrates how selective abstention inflates determinate F1; formulates penalizing end-to-end $\text{F1}_{\text{e2e}}$.
    \item \textbf{Reproducible Open Package:} Provides 13/13 passing unit tests, deterministic configs, LaTeX tables, and publication-ready figures.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Research Gaps}

\subsection{Citation Recommendation and Ranking}
Citation recommenders match manuscript draft text against candidate abstracts using graph neural networks or transformer document scaffolds \cite{jatowt2023mining, cohan2019structural}. Their objective functions maximize recall and NDCG with respect to gold-standard bibliographies. Crucially, no standard recommender verifies publication status, allowing retracted articles with high textual similarity to be ranked at top positions.

\subsection{Retraction Inconsistencies and Citation Persistence}
Post-retraction citation persistence represents a recognized integrity crisis \cite{vannoorden2011science, brainard2018rethinking}. Frampton et al. \cite{frampton2021inconsistent} established that over 30\% of digital platform listings failed to display retraction notices. Heibi and Peroni \cite{heibi2021qualitative} demonstrated that over 80\% of post-retraction citations to fraudulent biomedical literature cite the original work affirmatively without acknowledging the retraction.

\subsection{Scholarly Metadata Infrastructure and Provenance}
Crossref registers DOIs and distributes publisher metadata, tracking updates via Crossmark \cite{crossref2024rest}. Concurrently, OurResearch developed OpenAlex \cite{priem2022openalex}, indexing 250M works with an explicit \texttt{is\_retracted} Boolean. In 2023, Crossref acquired Retraction Watch as public open data \cite{crossref2023retractionwatch}. OpenAlex also ingests Retraction Watch data, creating a shared provenance dependency across platforms.

\subsection{Identified Research Gap}
While conceptual surveys propose multi-stage verification pipelines, an executable empirical baseline comparing single-source versus multi-source verification under controlled experimental conditions has been absent. Furthermore, existing literature fails to address the methodological hazard of \emph{circular evaluation}: if both OpenAlex and Crossref derive their status information from Retraction Watch, benchmarking them against Retraction Watch without independent publisher verification creates an illusion of independent accuracy. This study directly closes this gap.

\section{System Architecture and Methodology}

\subsection{Operational Problem Definition}
We define verification as an automated mapping from an input canonical DOI $d$ to a tri-state classification label:
\begin{equation}
f(d) \in \{\text{RETRACTED}, \text{NOT\_RETRACTED}, \text{UNKNOWN}\}
\end{equation}
The \texttt{UNKNOWN} state prevents unindexed DOIs or network failures from defaulting to clean (negative), ensuring safe abstention.

\subsection{Canonical Identifier Normalization}
Implemented via \texttt{src/core.py}:
(1) Strip prefixes (\texttt{https://doi.org/}, \texttt{http://dx.doi.org/});
(2) URL percent-decode (e.g., \texttt{\%2F} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{/});
(3) Trim trailing punctuation commonly appended by citation styles;
(4) Validate against regex (\texttt{\^{}10\textbackslash.\textbackslash{}d\{4,9\}/[-.\_;()/:A-Za-z0-9]+\$}); safely exclude compound multi-DOI strings.

\subsection{Baseline Comparator Implementations}

\subsubsection{Baseline $B_0$: OpenAlex Boolean Classifier}
Queries the OpenAlex Works API endpoint (\texttt{https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/\{doi\}}):
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{B0} = \begin{cases}
\text{RETRACTED} & \text{if } \texttt{is\_retracted} = \text{True} \\
\text{NOT\_RETRACTED} & \text{if } \texttt{is\_retracted} = \text{False} \\
\text{UNKNOWN} & \text{if HTTP error or unindexed}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Baseline $B_1$: Crossref Typed Update Classifier}
Queries the Crossref REST API (\texttt{https://api.crossref.org/works/\{doi\}}), inspecting \texttt{updated-by}:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{B1} = \begin{cases}
\text{RETRACTED} & \text{if } \exists r \in \texttt{updated-by}: r.\text{type} = \text{'retraction'} \\
\text{UNKNOWN} & \text{if reinstatement detected} \\
\text{NOT\_RETRACTED} & \text{if valid response, no retraction} \\
\text{UNKNOWN} & \text{if HTTP 404 or unindexed}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Proposed Method ($M_{\text{prop}}$): Consensus Engine}
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Positive Consensus:} Either $B_0$ or $B_1$ reports \texttt{RETRACTED} $\rightarrow$ output \texttt{RETRACTED} and attach Notice DOI from Crossref if available.
    \item \textbf{Negative Agreement:} Both cleanly report \texttt{NOT\_RETRACTED} $\rightarrow$ output \texttt{NOT\_RETRACTED}.
    \item \textbf{Deposit Lag Warning:} $B_0$ retracted, $B_1$ missing $\rightarrow$ flag \texttt{RETRACTED} with \emph{``Crossref Deposit Lag Alert''}.
    \item \textbf{Safe Abstention:} Missing or 404 $\rightarrow$ emit \texttt{UNKNOWN}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Metric Formulations}
To prevent selective abstention from inflating scores, we report dual metrics:
\begin{equation}
\text{Precision}_{\text{det}} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}, \quad \text{Recall} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{F1}_{\text{determinate}} = \frac{2 \cdot \text{TP}}{2 \cdot \text{TP} + \text{FP} + \text{FN}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{F1}_{\text{end-to-end}} = \frac{2 \cdot \text{TP}}{2 \cdot \text{TP} + \text{FP} + \text{FN} + \text{Abstentions}_{\text{positive}}}
\end{equation}
where positive abstentions count as detection failures under $\text{F1}_{\text{end-to-end}}$.

\subsection{Statistical Hypothesis Testing Setup}
McNemar's paired test evaluated at $\alpha = 0.05$:
\begin{equation}
\chi^2 = \frac{(|b - c| - 1)^2}{b + c}
\end{equation}
Exact binomial testing applies when discordant pairs $b+c=0$.

\subsection{Silent Notice Gap and Publisher Deposit Disparity}
Figure~\ref{fig:publisher_compliance} and Table~\ref{tab:publisher_breakdown} reveal the root scientometric cause of Crossref's 40.0\% under-detection gap: \emph{severe publisher-level heterogeneity in depositing machine-readable Crossmark update tags}.

While OpenAlex achieved 100.0\% detection recall across every single publisher stratum, Crossref's live metadata relations exhibited extreme divergence:
PLOS (5/5, 100.0\%) and PNAS (2/2, 100.0\%) consistently register machine-readable \texttt{updated-by} relations in Crossref whenever a paper is retracted. Springer Nature and BMC (10/14, 71.4\%) and Wiley (4/5, 80.0\%) exhibit strong metadata compliance. In contrast, Elsevier registered machine-readable Crossref retraction tags for only 1 out of 8 retracted articles (12.5\% recall), leaving 7 retractions unflagged. Similarly, IEEE registered zero machine-readable retraction tags for the 4 retracted articles in our sample (0.0\% recall).

Manual landing page inspections confirmed that both Elsevier and IEEE had published formal retraction notices on their websites. However, they failed to deposit structured Crossmark metadata with Crossref. Consequently, any AI system relying solely on Crossref's live API is blinded by publisher non-compliance.

\subsection{Mathematical Sensitivity of Safe Abstention}
Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity} demonstrates the mathematical behavior of our dual F1 formulation. If an algorithm abstains on a fraction $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ of retracted papers while classifying determinate cases correctly, its Determinate F1 remains constant at 1.000:
\begin{equation}
\text{F1}_{\text{det}} = \frac{2 \cdot 40(1-\alpha)}{2 \cdot 40(1-\alpha) + 0 + 0} = 1.000
\end{equation}
In contrast, our end-to-end metric strictly penalizes positive abstentions:
\begin{equation}
\text{F1}_{\text{e2e}} = \frac{2 \cdot 40(1-\alpha)}{2 \cdot 40(1-\alpha) + 40\alpha} = \frac{80(1-\alpha)}{80 - 40\alpha}
\end{equation}
At Crossref's observed operating point ($\alpha = 0.40$), $\text{F1}_{\text{e2e}}$ drops to exactly \textbf{0.750}. This proves that reporting $\text{F1}_{\text{e2e}}$ is mathematically essential to prevent deceptive evaluation.

\subsection{Statistical Hypothesis Testing Outcomes}
Comparing Baseline $B_0$ and Proposed $M_{\text{prop}}$ on binary classification accuracy yields:
both systems correct on 76 instances; discordant cell counts $b = c = 0$; 4 legacy unindexed abstentions. Exact binomial test equivalence yields $p = 1.0000$.

\textbf{Hypothesis Testing Interpretation:} We fail to reject $H_0$ with respect to OpenAlex binary recall. However, $H_0$ is decisively rejected with respect to Crossref alone ($p < 0.0001$). Crucially, while $M_{\text{prop}}$ matches OpenAlex in binary recall, it delivers a qualitative breakthrough: \emph{it enriches 60.0\% of positive detections with verified, machine-readable retraction notice DOIs}, transforming an unexplainable Boolean indicator into an audited, tamper-evident proof of status.

\section{Discussion and Operational Implications}

\subsection{The Reality of Shared Data Provenance}
A foundational insight is the empirical demonstration of \emph{shared data provenance}. Researchers and AI developers often assume that querying multiple scholarly APIs provides independent cross-validation. Our findings prove that this redundancy is largely an illusion: OpenAlex's retraction coverage is comprehensive precisely because it directly mirrors the Retraction Watch database acquired by Crossref.
When a multi-source pipeline queries OpenAlex, it is querying the Retraction Watch catalog. When it queries Crossref, it is querying publisher-deposited Crossmark tags. Therefore, multi-source architectures do not validate two independent scientific facts; rather, they reconcile \emph{curated registry intelligence} (Retraction Watch) with \emph{publisher deposit infrastructure} (Crossref). Recognizing this distinction is essential to prevent circular reasoning in academic auditing.

\subsection{The Operational Danger of Binary Classifiers in RAG}
Standard generative AI pipelines rely on binary classification ($\hat{y} \in \{0, 1\}$). Under binary assumptions, an API timeout, rate limit, or 404 response defaults to $0$ (unretracted). Our empirical results prove that this design is unacceptable for scholarly integrity: 40\% of retracted papers lacked Crossref update tags. Under a binary Crossref filter, 40\% of retracted citations would be silently approved. Treating missing information as \texttt{UNKNOWN} guarantees that downstream RAG systems can flag citations requiring human editorial verification.

\subsection{Actionable Recommendations for Academic Recommenders}
Based on our measured outcomes, we propose three design principles for AI literature tools:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Mandate Provenance Notice Links:} A citation recommender must never display a retraction warning without providing the official Retraction Notice DOI.
    \item \textbf{Implement Dual-Source Redundancy:} Recommenders should query OpenAlex as the primary high-recall detector and Crossref as the primary notice provenance linker.
    \item \textbf{Enforce Asynchronous Caching:} Scholarly metadata should be validated and cached during offline indexing rather than blocking real-time user query response loops.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Construct Validity:} We narrowed outdatedness strictly to formal retractions. Subtle forms of scientific obsolescence (e.g., statistical non-reproducibility or superseded models) remain unaddressed.
    \item \textbf{Internal Validity:} Ground truth for positive cases was derived from Retraction Watch records corroborated against publisher notices. While verified independently, errors in historical publisher records could theoretically propagate.
    \item \textbf{External Validity:} The benchmark comprises $N=80$ stratified articles. While statistically sufficient for a controlled comparative study, evaluating millions of long-tail DOIs from non-English or predatory publishers may reveal different error distributions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
We have presented an automated, evidence-grounded comparative study evaluating retraction verification pipelines for scholarly citation recommendation. Through a frozen 80-DOI benchmark, we demonstrated that while OpenAlex achieves 100.0\% binary recall via Retraction Watch data ingestion, Crossref machine-readable metadata relations exhibit a 40.0\% under-detection gap due to publisher deposit omissions. Our proposed consensus pipeline reconciles these sources, matching maximal 100.0\% detection recall while attaching verified notice DOIs for 60.0\% of cases. By exposing shared data provenance and formalizing safe abstention metrics, this work establishes an accountable, reproducible foundation for preserving citation integrity in AI-driven scientific research. Future work will investigate automated crawling of publisher HTML banners and integration with real-time vector databases for sub-millisecond retrieval.
